# The Notebook

The ship's chief relic. The original is kept in a reliquary behind the hub altar. A likeness of one page is displayed under glass in every schoolroom.

## What it actually was

A pocket field notebook, paper, carried in the chest pocket of a dive suit. Miriam kept it through the whole 2075 expedition. Most of it is ordinary: equipment checks, a sketch of the sonar profile, remarks about the coffee, at least one unkind line about Dunmore. Two entries matter.

- **The question,** written four days before the dive: *You date the ship, not the seafloor. When did it arrive?*
- **The Descent page:** a time, and three lines. *Grown, not made. Nothing settles on it. It gives.*

The stain on one corner is coffee. She put it down on a wet mess table.

## How it was passed down

1. **A prop.** On her world tour Miriam held up the Descent page. She sounded like a scientist and this was her exhibit: a note made at the time, before she could have known what it meant. That image went everywhere.
2. **One page only.** She never published the rest. Nobody was funding scholarship on a linguist's field notes while governments were falling. Your Bible says people stopped caring about details once they had the headline. The notebook is the first example.
3. **A custody fight.** After her death the movement's religious authority wanted a relic and its media controller wanted a reproduction in every home. Both won. The original was sealed. The one page was copied.
4. **The likeness.** The copies were made on Earth by a printing process the ships did not carry. Aboard, nobody can make another. Each schoolroom's copy is itself now three hundred years old.
5. **The Opening.** The reliquary is opened once in a generation, to confirm the original is sound. It is opened to the Descent page and closed again. No one alive has seen any other page.

## How the meaning changed

| The fact | What Year 304 teaches |
| --- | --- |
| Three short field notes | **The Three Lines**, read as the Trinity. *Grown, not made* is the Son, by echo of the Creed's "begotten, not made." *Nothing settles on it* is the Father's holiness. *It gives* is the Spirit, the giver of life. |
| She wrote little because she was cold, busy and exact | She was too moved to write more |
| A coffee stain | The water of the Descent |
| "You date the ship, not the seafloor" | A ship proverb, "Date the ship, not the sea," meaning judge a thing by when it came to you and not by what surrounds it. Its source is forgotten. |
| "Grown, not made" as a description of a biomineral | Proof that faith was already rising in an unbeliever |

## Why it mutated this way

- **Scripture was needed and a field note was what there was.** The scrolls themselves can only be read by one dead woman. The Notebook is the only text in her hand that ordinary people can read whole. It carries more weight than it was built for.
- **Three of anything will become the Trinity** on a ship that teaches the Trinity through physics from the age of five.
- **Brevity invites filling.** A page with three lines on it is mostly blank, and people write on blank space.
- **The first line really does echo the Creed.** That is not an invention of the catechists. The open question is whether the echo was faith or just the furniture of a mind that had been to seminary. The ship has decided. Wren has not.

## What is still unknown

- What is on the other pages.
- Whether the Descent page has anything on it that the likeness does not show: a pressed-through mark, a line begun and stopped. Draft 2 has her hold the pen over a fourth line and not write it.
- Whether anyone at an Opening has ever turned the page.

## Why the story wants it

Your Bible's central problem is that nobody can check Miriam's reading of the scrolls. The Notebook is that problem made into an object the ship can stand in front of. It is the one piece of her that anyone can read, and they have been reading it wrong for three hundred years, or possibly right.
