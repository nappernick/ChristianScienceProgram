# The Vigil of the Descent

An annual rite. The ship sits in darkness for as long as the 2075 dive took to reach the bottom, while a Reader recites the dive and the people give the replies.

## What actually happened (2075)

Three people in a submersible took about seventy minutes to sink two thousand meters. The light went from blue to dark blue to nothing. They found a chamber and called the ship. A pilot bumped the cylinder with a camera housing. A linguist wrote three lines in a notebook.

## The original sources

1. **The vehicle recorder.** Cabin audio and telemetry for the whole dive. The fullest record and the least flattering: Miriam asking whether she needs a doctor or coffee, Hess's rule, the bump.
2. **Miriam's own account.** Written for the first public briefings. Precise, dry, a scientist's report. "First blue, then the memory of blue" is her phrase. So is the rock "asked to make room."
3. **The Notebook.** One page.
4. **Hess and Natasha, later.** Their accounts do not match each other in places.

## How it changed, stage by stage

| Stage | What people had | What it became |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First years after the dive | The recorder audio, released in edited form | **Descent Day.** An anniversary broadcast of the audio in real time. People listened at home with the lights low, because there was nothing to look at. |
| Coalition years | A founding story was needed | The broadcast became a public event. Listeners began murmuring the famous lines along with the recording. These are the first replies. |
| War and after | The audio contained too much: a founder who hesitated, a pilot who nearly dropped the arm | The recording was withdrawn. A written **Reading** replaced it, drawn from Miriam's account and edited by the movement's religious authority. A person now read it aloud. |
| Departure | Ships carried the Reading, not the recording | The first shipboard Vigils were held in the main chapel, under weight, with lamps dimmed. |
| About Year 60 | The reactor needed an annual inspection that required shedding most of the ship's electrical load for an hour | A practical abbot scheduled the inspection for Descent Day, so the dark hour served both. Within two generations nobody remembered there had been an engineering reason. |
| About Year 90 | The main chapel could not be fully darkened during load-shed; the hub could | The Vigil moved to the hub. Weightlessness was explained afterward as stillness. |
| Years 150 to 250 | Distance from Earth, and the intensifying of all shipboard practice | The shutters, the tethered rings, the Silence, and the rule that the Descent may never be shortened. |

## Why it changed this way

- **A founder who did not know is unaffordable.** In 2075 Miriam did not know what she had found. It took her three years to read any of it. A civilization that cannot turn back needs her to have known in the hour. So: "And she knew."
- **The bump became a hand.** A camera housing is not an image anyone can hold. A hand is. The change was made in the first written Reading and never questioned.
- **"Warm" is a true fact in the wrong place.** The material is alive. That was discovered years later by scientists. A sermon in the coalition years called it "warm as a living thing," as a figure of speech. The figure was written into the Reading as an event.
- **Engineering became liturgy.** Your world says everything aboard is liturgy. The reverse also happens: a maintenance window acquires a theology and then forgets it was ever a maintenance window.
- **Nobody aboard has seen a sea.** "Eleven hundred meters" is a line in a poem. The depths are recited as a ladder of holy numbers. No one could say what a meter of water is.

## What survives underneath

- The duration is still exact, because the engineers still need the hour.
- The joke about the Silent One ("it was more than he was used to give") is a true detail from the recorder that somebody liked too much to cut.
- The recording itself. Whether a copy was carried, and where it is, is an open question worth keeping open.
