# The Reader's Cure

The institution behind Sister Perpetua. When a bright child is heard to waver at the Vigil, the catechists put that child's name forward to be Reader the following year.

## Where it came from

Your Bible describes what Miriam built after the war: dissent treated as contamination, loyalty frameworks, purity tests. It also says those structures looked reasonable at the time. This is one of them, three hundred years on.

1. **Earth, the consolidation.** Public doubt about the mission was treated as a threat to it. The tools were blunt: removal from posts, exclusion from the fleet lists.
2. **Departure.** The same tools came aboard. They did not work. On Earth a dissenter could be left behind. On a ship there is nowhere to put anyone, and every pair of hands is needed.
3. **The first generations.** Open doubters were "set apart": moved to solitary work at the far end of the lower arm, kept out of the rings. It cost the ship skilled people and it made martyrs. Children noticed who was missing.
4. **About Year 120.** A catechist whose name is not kept observed that the children most likely to waver were the cleverest, and that the cleverest children made the best Readers. She began putting waverers forward. It was not policy. It was one teacher's kindness.
5. **By Year 200.** It was the unwritten rule of every arm. Setting apart still exists, for those who speak. It is now rare, because few get that far.

## How it works

- A Reader spends a year learning the Vigil by heart and is trusted with things ordinary crew never see.
- A Reader stands alone in the dark and hears three thousand people answer.
- "It is very hard to hear a gap in your own voice when it is the only voice."

Most who go through it come out as the ship's steadiest believers, or its steadiest performers of belief. The catechists do not ask which.

## What it has done to the ship

For nearly two centuries the people chosen to lead the liturgy have been chosen from among those who doubted it. The catechists, the Readers and much of the council were all children who once went quiet at a reply. They lead very well. They know exactly where the weak places are, because they have stood on them.

This is your Hum. It is what it sounds like when a whole institution is staffed by people trying hard not to hear something, each of them believing they are nearly alone in it.

## Why it mutated this way

- **You cannot exile anyone from a ship.** Punishment had to turn into absorption.
- **Small populations cannot waste talent.** The clever child is needed, so a use had to be found for her that was also a leash.
- **It was built by people who had been through it.** Each generation of catechists did to the next what had been done to them, and experienced it as rescue.
- **It mirrors the founder.** Miriam bound her disciples by letting each one see a single crack in her certainty. The Cure binds children by letting a teacher admit, once, "I have always thought so too."

## Why the story wants it

- It makes the ship's control gentle, which is harder to fight and more interesting to read than cruelty.
- It gives Wren a path to the center that is also a trap.
- It means that when someone finally does speak from the Reader's place, a large part of the hub will already privately agree. What matters is whether they say so.
