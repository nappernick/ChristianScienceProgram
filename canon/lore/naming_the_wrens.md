# Naming the Wrens

Why a fifteen-year-old girl in Year 304 is called Wren, and why there are four Natashas in her year and more boys called Hess than the catechists can keep straight.

## The original facts

- The submersible was called the *Wren*, after Christopher Wren, the architect of St Paul's Cathedral. Hess told Miriam this on the second day out. She forgot who had told her.
- Natasha was the dive's lead scientist. Hess was the pilot. Hess is a surname. Nobody recorded his first name with any care.

## How the names spread

1. **The first wave.** After the find went public, children all over the Christian world were named Miriam.
2. **The restriction.** During the consolidation after the war, the Founder's name was withdrawn from ordinary use. The stated reason was reverence. The practical reason was that a population full of Miriams made the name ordinary, and an authority built on one woman could not afford that.
3. **The displacement.** Devotion that could not go to her name went to the names beside it. Natasha became the commonest girl's name of the departure generation. Hess, a surname, became a boy's given name.
4. **Wren.** A vessel is not a person, so the name was free to anyone. It was taken up first by the shipwrights who built the fleet. They knew the name came from a builder of churches, and they were building churches that flew.

## How it changed aboard

- **The Wrights kept it.** The guild that maintains the ship's structure descends from those shipwrights. Naming a daughter Wren is their custom. In Year 304 it marks a family as much as a surname would.
- **The architect faded.** Children are taught that a wren was a bird, and a boat, and before that a man who built churches. The man has no first name any more, no city and no cathedral. He is a sentence.
- **The bird became imaginary.** Nobody aboard has seen one. In children's drawings a wren is a small brown shape with a cross on its breast.
- **Hess became a type.** The liturgy calls the pilot "the Silent One." A boy named Hess is expected to be steady and to say little. The loud ones, like this year's Reader, are teased for it.
- **Natasha became the witness.** In the Vigil she is the one who "spoke of the warmth of the water." The name carries a sense of the person who was there and saw, and girls given it are told so.

## Why it went this way

- **A forbidden name pushes its weight onto its neighbors.** This is how saints' cults form.
- **A closed population reuses names until they turn into roles.** With three thousand people and one founding story, a name stops identifying a person and starts assigning a part.
- **Trades remember what liturgy forgets.** The Wrights still know the name is about building. It is the same pattern as the receiver crews and the Pause: the true origin survives as a guild habit.

## Why the story wants it

- A girl named for the vessel is, by her name, the thing that carries people down to look.
- "She had never seen a bird. She had never seen a boat. She had lived her whole life inside a church" depends on the bird and the boat having become words.
- Every Hess aboard, including her small brother, is named for a man the liturgy praises for silence. The real Hess is the one witness who could say what happened.
