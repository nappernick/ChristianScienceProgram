# The Pause

In the Vigil, after the Reader speaks each of the Founder's calls, the people keep silent for "a breath and a third" before giving the surface's reply.

## What it actually was

Physics. The submersible spoke to the ship by sound, and sound in cold seawater travels about fifteen hundred meters a second. At two thousand meters a spoken word took a second and a third to reach the surface, and the answer took a second and a third to come back. Every exchange on the recorder has that gap in it.

## How it was passed down

1. **By ear.** In the Descent Day broadcasts, people who murmured along with the recording learned the gap without being told about it. You cannot say the reply on time unless you wait.
2. **By habit.** When the recording was withdrawn and a Reader took over, the first Readers had grown up on the broadcasts. They left the gap because the lines sounded wrong without it.
3. **By rule.** Within a generation the gap was written into the rubric as "a second and a third." At this point somebody still knew why.
4. **By breath.** Aboard ship, catechists teaching very small children in a dark hub could not use a clock. They taught the count on the breath. By about Year 110 the catechism said "a breath and a third," and the older wording was gone.

## How it has drifted

A calm breath takes three or four seconds. A breath and a third is about five. The original lag was a second and a third each way. The Pause is now roughly four times as long as the thing it preserves, and nobody has checked, because nobody knows there is anything to check it against.

## The three explanations, in order of age

- **Coalition era:** a note in the printed program said the delay was the time sound took to travel through the water. Accurate.
- **War era:** "The Founder waited upon the Lord, and so do we." The physics was dropped because it made the moment sound like a telephone call.
- **Ship era:** "It is the length of the Pause," for children. "Waiting is a kind of trust," for those who ask twice.

## Why it mutated this way

- **The cause became unimaginable.** There is no sea aboard and no sound link. The explanation had nothing left to attach to.
- **A gap in a ritual attracts meaning.** People will not stand in silence for no reason. If the reason is lost they supply one.
- **It is useful.** Three thousand people holding a silence together and then speaking together is the strongest moment of unity in the year. No authority would give that up for the sake of accuracy.

## Who still knows

The people who work the long-range receivers live with a real lag every day: years, to Earth and to sister ships. Among them there is an old saying that the Pause is "the first lag," and a quiet theology that goes with it: all prayer is a message sent across a delay of unknown length. Sister Perpetua knows this version. It is the nearest thing aboard to the truth, and it survives as a trade superstition.

## Why the story wants it

It is one fact doing three jobs. It is hard science in 2075. It is a fossil in Year 304. And it is the book's argument in small: the ship is a message that has been sent, and nobody aboard knows whether there is a reply coming.
