# Tobias Adeyemi: Flaws

## The flaw

He cannot keep a thing to himself, and he needs it to be true badly enough to round it up.

## Why he is flawed this way

He is the son who did not become a pastor. He has spent his adult life half-apologizing for loving machines more than pulpits. A discovery like this, made on his ship, on a vehicle he maintains, is the first time his work and his father's have pointed at the same thing. He is not going to examine it carefully. He is going to carry it home.

And where he comes from, telling is the virtuous act. Nobody ever taught him that some true things should wait.

## How it shows

- He hears "it reads like bone" and by evening it is "it's alive."
- He takes Miriam's certainty for shared faith. It is not.
- He tells his church group. He does not think of it as a leak. He thinks of it as Sunday.

## How it moves the story

1. **The news gets out as testimony, not espionage.** Carrow builds a wall against rival companies and foreign governments. It is breached by a voice message to forty people in Peckham. The world first hears of the scrolls as a piece of good news from a believer, and that framing never comes off.
2. **He gives Miriam her first failed sharing.** Your Bible says the worst of these are with believers. He is why she first learns that what she carries cannot be handed over, and he is too kind for her to be angry at.
3. **He rounds up, and she lets it stand.** His version is warmer and simpler than the facts. It is also more useful. The first time she hears someone repeat his version and does not correct it, something has started.
4. **He is the first test of what she will do to a person.** Carrow wants him punished for the leak. Miriam can protect him or use him. Either choice tells the reader who she is becoming, and the liturgy three hundred years later will have an opinion about him too.
5. **He supplies heat.** Every other person aboard is cold. Scenes with him in them have someone who laughs.
