# The Ensemble: how the cast is built to collide

The character folders describe each person alone. This file describes them as a set: why these people, what each is for, and which pairings make scenes.

## 1. One question, and every character is an answer to it

The book's question is: **what do you do with a truth you cannot prove to anyone else?**

Every character is one answer. Put any two in a room and they disagree for a reason that matters to the book.

| Character | Archetype | Their answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miriam | The Seeker who becomes the Ruler | Make everyone act on it |
| Natasha | The Witness | Record it exactly and refuse to say what it means |
| Hess | The Ferryman | Keep quiet and keep faith with what you saw |
| Iyer | The Judge | Take it to the room and let the room decide |
| Dunmore | The Skeptic | If only one person holds it, it is not knowledge |
| Carrow | The Gatekeeper | Own it before anyone else does |
| Tobias | The Herald | Tell everybody, gladly, at once |
| Wren | The Orphan who becomes the Truth-teller | Hold it unsaid and learn to bear not knowing |
| Sister Perpetua | The Mentor at the threshold | Manage it, for other people's sake |
| Anselm | The Scapegoat | Say it aloud and pay |
| The Reader (the loud Hess) | The Zealot | Say it louder so you cannot hear yourself |

## 2. Two axes, so conflict is predictable

- **Certainty:** needs to know, or can live without knowing.
- **Disclosure:** tells, or withholds.

| | Tells | Withholds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Needs to know** | Early Miriam, Dunmore, Tobias, Iyer | Late Miriam, Carrow, the Reader |
| **Can live without knowing** | Natasha, Anselm | Hess, Wren, Sister Perpetua |

How to use the grid:

- **Opposite corners fight.** Miriam against Hess. Carrow against Natasha. Dunmore against Wren, across three centuries.
- **Same corner, opposite conclusions, is the sharpest fight.** Dunmore and Tobias both need to know and both say so loudly. One says no and one says yes. Early Miriam sits between them.
- **Only two people move.** Miriam goes from telling to withholding. Wren goes from withholding to telling. They cross. That crossing is the shape of the book.

## 3. Mirrors

Each major figure has someone who shares a trait and shows a different outcome.

| Pair | Shared trait | What the mirror shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miriam and Dunmore | Rigor and certainty | Who she was before the scrolls, still talking |
| Miriam and Wren | Carrying something alone | Miriam had to learn it. Wren was born knowing it, because of the world Miriam made |
| Miriam and Perpetua | Binding people by showing one crack | The founder's method, alive in a schoolteacher |
| Natasha and Hess | Were there, saw it | One testifies precisely, one barely speaks, and they do not agree |
| Iyer and Carrow | Protecting a truth | The room against the vault |
| Tobias and Anselm | Saying it aloud in public | Spoken belief is rewarded. Spoken doubt is punished. It is the same act |

## 4. Voice rules

So that a line of dialogue can be assigned without a tag.

| Character | How they talk | When cornered | Never says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Miriam | Whole sentences. Repeats a finding word for word. Early on, explains too much | Becomes more precise | "I feel" |
| Natasha | Answers the literal question. Level voice when it costs her | "That is all I am prepared to say" | A guess |
| Hess | Under ten words. States his conditions in advance | Switches to the recorder voice | Why |
| Iyer | Begins by conceding, then holds. "My number" | Calls for the room | "I decided" |
| Dunmore | Diagnoses. Asks questions he thinks are closed | Goes silent | "I was wrong" |
| Carrow | Short imperatives | Answers a question with a question | What he wants |
| Tobias | Stories, laughter, "God" used as an everyday word | Testifies | "I shouldn't say" |
| Wren | Asks once, then watches | Says the expected thing very clearly | What she has noticed |
| Sister Perpetua | Corrects gently. Confides one true thing and closes the subject | Gives a practical instruction | "You must" |
| The Reader | Loud, exact, word-perfect | Gets louder | Anything of his own |

**Temperature rule:** every scene has at least one hot speaker and one cold one. Hot: Tobias, Dunmore, Iyer when her date is doubted, the Reader. Cold: Miriam, Hess, Carrow, Natasha, Perpetua.

## 6. Three positions, not two

Every major decision should have at least three people wanting different things. Two positions make an argument. Three make a scene, because any two can combine against the third.

Example, the decision to lift the cylinder: Iyer wants to wait, Carrow wants to hide it, Miriam wants it now, and Natasha is the one asking what it will cost.

## 7. The fault I found, and the fix

Before this file, everyone on the 2075 ship was the same person in a different job: reserved, competent, secular. Miriam, Natasha, Hess, Iyer, Dunmore and Carrow would all underplay the same moment in the same way.

What was missing:

- A believer. Your Bible says Miriam's worst failed attempts to share are with believers, and there was none aboard.
- A talker.
- Warmth and youth.
- Someone who makes an honest mistake.

**Tobias Adeyemi** fills all four. He is a twenty-four-year-old deck technician and the first person to be glad about what they found. See his folder.

## 8. What I am deliberately not doing

- **Not making Hess a believer.** His silence is more useful if nobody, including the reader, can tell what he thinks.
- **Not giving Miriam a villain.** Her opponents are each right about something.
- **Not giving anyone her interior.** After the translation, no other character gets to understand her. That isolation is the engine of the Bible and the cast is built to keep it running.
