# Wren: Flaws

## The flaw

She tests things by withdrawing and watching, and she keeps what she finds to herself. She mistakes silence for honesty.

## Why she is flawed this way

She was born into a place with no outside. Every question she asked aloud came back with an answer that closed the subject. A child in that position learns to stop asking and start observing. Your Bible says Miriam had to learn, painfully, that some things cannot be shared. Wren has known it since she was five. She has the instinct Miriam lacked, and she has it because of the world Miriam built.

There is also pride in it. She believes she is the only one who notices. She is wrong, and Sister Perpetua tells her so.

## How it shows

- The silent reply. An experiment, not a protest.
- She has carried the "careful handwriting" thought for months without a word.
- She would sooner be thought dutiful than be known.

## How it moves the story

1. **Her experiments get her noticed.** The gap at the Vigil is what puts her name forward. Her attempt to step outside the sound moves her to the center of it.
2. **Her secrecy makes her a dangerous person to give access to.** As Reader she will see things. She will not report what she sees. She will sit on it, and the reader will sit on it with her.
3. **She repeats Miriam's isolation from the other end.** Miriam carried something alone because nobody could follow her. Wren carries something alone because nobody is allowed to. The parallel lets the two timelines talk to each other.
4. **Her flaw has to break for the book to end.** At some point she must say a thing aloud, from the Reader's place, in front of three thousand people. Everything before that is the pressure building.
