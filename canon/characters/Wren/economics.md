# Wren: Economics

The ship has no money. It has allocation: rations, quarters, and work assignments, set by guild and standing.

- **Her family's standing:** middle. The Wrights are respected because hull work is dangerous, and poor in comfort because of where they live and when they work.
- **Her father's work:** exterior welding. It carries the highest injury rate aboard and the highest radiation dose.
- **Her mother's work:** the wheat decks. Steady and safe.
- **Her own path, before this year:** an apprenticeship with the Wrights, starting with weld preparation.

## What being Reader brings a family

- A place in the first ring at every Vigil for life.
- A larger bread allocation in the Reader year.
- **The Reader's parent is moved off exterior shifts for that year,** by custom, so that nothing can happen to them before the Vigil.

## What this does to her choices

- Accepting takes her father off the hull for a year. Refusing puts him back on it. Nobody has said this to her. Her mother will not need to.
- Refusing would also cost her brother, who is six and would grow up as the boy whose sister said no.
- The honor is real, the door is closing, and the price of not walking through it is paid by people she loves. That is how the ship arranges things.
