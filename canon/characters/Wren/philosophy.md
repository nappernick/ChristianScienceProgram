# Wren: Philosophy

She is fifteen, so this is a set of instincts more than a system.

- **She does not disbelieve. She cannot tell.** What she wants is to be allowed not to know, and the ship has no place to stand while not knowing.
- **She trusts what holds its interval.** A thing that keeps happening the same way whether or not anyone is watching is more real to her than a thing everyone says together.
- **A true thing should not need everyone to say it at once.** She has never put it in those words. It is why the replies bother her.
- **Two readings can both stand.** Her thought at the glass, that the Founder's first line could be the Creed rising in her or could be a tired woman describing what she saw, is the first idea she has had that is neither for the Founder nor against her.
- **The waiting is the honest part.** The breath and a third after you have spoken and before you know.

## Why this matters for the book

Miriam could not live with not knowing and burned the world to escape it. Wren is the first person in three hundred years to find not knowing bearable. The ships are flying toward an answer, and she is the one character equipped to receive whatever it turns out to be.
