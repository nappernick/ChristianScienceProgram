# Dr. Alistair Dunmore: Choices

| Situation | What he does | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any surprising result | States the dullest explanation first, in public | It has been right forty-one times |
| The evidence turns against him | Goes quiet. Does not concede | He has no practice at it |
| Alone at night | Reruns the numbers himself, honestly | He is a real scientist |
| He privately confirms Iyer's date | Tells nobody | Conceding in public is beyond him |
| The object is put in his hands | Says exactly what it is | He will not lie about a rock |
| Asked to accept Miriam's reading | Refuses, on the ground that nobody can check it | His oldest principle, and this time it is correct |
| Offered money or position to be quiet | Laughs | He needs nothing |
| Shut out of the room | Keeps talking, to fewer and fewer people | The fight is what he has |

## The line he will not cross

He will never say a thing is true because it would be convenient, kind, or safe. That is his one virtue and the story should let him keep it.
