# Dr. Alistair Dunmore: Flaws

## The flaw

Contempt. He cannot make an objection without making it about the person he is objecting to, and he cannot change his mind in front of anyone.

## Why he is flawed this way

The debunking at thirty-four rewarded him for cruelty. He was right, he was savage, and the field made him its gatekeeper. Forty-one correct refusals since have taught him that his first instinct is reliable and that the people on the other side are fools or frauds. He has no practice at being wrong and no model for doing it gracefully.

## How it shows

- "Intrusion from an older stratum," said like a diagnosis.
- He goes quiet instead of conceding. His silence in the comms shack is the nearest he gets to an apology.
- He needs to be the adult in the room, so he resents anyone the room turns toward.

## How it moves the story

1. **He gives the reader the best mundane explanation, early and every time.** Contamination, multipath, coincidence. The story's central question is whether Miriam found God or found a coincidence, and the reader needs to have heard the skeptical case from someone competent before they hear hers.
2. **He makes the correct objection unlikable.** The single most important criticism of Miriam, that only she can read the deep layer, will come first from a man everyone is glad to dismiss. That is how a true thing gets ignored.
3. **He raises the cost of doubt for everyone else.** After the find, agreeing with Dunmore means standing next to Dunmore. Iyer will not do it. Natasha will hesitate.
4. **He is what Miriam used to be.** Each time he speaks, she hears the dinner-party voice she now finds humiliating. Her eventual treatment of dissent begins as an inability to listen to this one man.
