# Dr. Kamala Iyer: History

*Chief scientist of the 2075 survey. First name is a placeholder.*

- **Born** 2017, Chennai. 58 at the time of the dive.
- **Field:** geochronology. Her specialty is argon-argon dating of young volcanic rock, the hardest end of the method, where the signal is small and the arguments are long.
- **The eight years.** In her early thirties she redated an Icelandic eruption sequence that the field had placed forty thousand years too young. She was right. It took eight years for the field to say so. Two of the people who had mocked her retired without ever saying it.
- **What that taught her:** a correct number, defended for long enough, wins. Also that being right early looks exactly like being wrong until the day it doesn't.
- **The comedown.** Research funding collapsed into corporate contract work in the 2060s. She now runs commercial seabed surveys for mining consortia: competent, unglamorous, well paid. She thinks of it as a step down and has never said so aloud.
- **On this expedition.** She ran the basalt dates herself, got two hundred thousand years, ran them again, and spent three dinners defending the figure against Dunmore.
- **At the dive.** She is the voice on the acoustic link. She is the one who says "Past that it's your call, Dr. Calder."
