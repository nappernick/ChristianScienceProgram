# Sister Perpetua: Economics

- **What she owns:** nothing. She is under vows. Her habit, her cell near the hub, and her ration come from the order.
- **What she controls:** the names that go forward for Reader from the port arm. On a ship without money, that is the most valuable thing a person can give a family.
- **What families do about it:** send bread, mend her habit unasked, steer bright children into her schoolroom. She refuses the gifts and notices the children.
- **What her order depends on:** steady Readers. The catechists' standing with the council rests on the Vigil going well every year.
- **What a failed Reader costs:** her judgment, the order's reputation, and the practice itself. If a child she chose speaks from the Reader's place, the argument for absorbing doubters instead of setting them apart is lost for a generation.

## What this does to her choices

- She has every incentive to choose well and to make sure the choice holds.
- Her kindness and her institution's interest point the same way, so she has never had to find out which one she would follow.
- She is not corrupt. She is invested, and Wren is the investment.
