# Miriam Calder: Psychology

*The Bible is the source. This file sorts what it fixes and marks what it leaves open.*

## Fixed by the Bible

- **Certainty is her operating system.** Her atheism was not a belief she held. It was how she thought. It stood on three independent grounds: deductive, historical, personal.
- **She sees mechanisms.** Who has power, who wants what, which lever moves which outcome.
- **Her first instinct is to share.** Twenty years as a scholar and, before that, seminary: both taught her that you take what you have found to other people. She has no instinct for carrying a thing alone. She learns it later, through failed attempts to be understood.
- **When the scrolls demolish her, the result is fury, not humility.** At herself, the church, the academy, the world, and God.
- **Her faith returns like a sentence being passed.** She acknowledges the authority. She does not love it.
- **The third ground survives.** God may be real and still not good.
- **When doubt returns, she acts.** An irreversible act forces certainty back. Each one makes the question harder to ask.
- **The question she will not ask:** if He is not real, what am I?

## Fixed by the chapters

- She can let her mind go slack during the parts of an operation that do not need her.
- She reads context automatically, the way other people read faces.
- She learned when to share and when to wait from a teacher who disliked her.

## Left open by the Bible

- What happened to her, or to someone she loved. She never spoke of it. Accounts conflict.
- Whether the vulnerability she later shows her disciples is real, performed, or both.
