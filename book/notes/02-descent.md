
EDITORIAL NOTES

What this chapter is trying to do:
- Physical immersion in the descent. The reader should feel the pressure, the dark, the duration, the boredom-that-isn't-boredom.
- Hess as a character established in minimal strokes — competent, silent, respectful of their work.
- The moment the cavity appears on sonar BEFORE they see it with eyes — data first, then reality. That's how deep-sea work actually operates.
- Mariana's reaction is NOT dramatic. She goes quiet. She looks. She recognizes the pattern. That's it. The drama lives in the understatement.
- The Antikythera callback at the end — she recognizes the tone of voice because she's heard it before. She's been in this moment once already. That connects Chapter 1 to Chapter 2 without repeating information.
- "We need to call the surface" as the closing line. Not a cliffhanger. A professional judgment delivered with the weight of someone who understands the implications.

What I'm uncertain about:
- Length. This might be too short or too long for the material.
- Hess. He might need more or less. Right now he's functional — the silent competent pilot. He could become a real character or stay furniture.
- The description of the cavity. I tried to keep it grounded in what sonar actually shows rather than describing it like a movie camera. But it might be too technical or not technical enough.
- Mariana's internal experience at the moment of recognition. I kept it minimal — "something between recognition and vertigo" — but you might want more or less interiority here.

What to write next in Chapter 3:
- The call to the surface. What they say and what the surface says back.
- The decision to attempt extraction or at least closer examination.
- The first physical contact with the cylinder.
- The moment someone realizes the material is biological.
